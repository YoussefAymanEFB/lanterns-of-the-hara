# The month ahead · فوانيس الحارة

A draft plan for the 30 days, for review. Day 1 stays simple and straight on purpose: one neighbour, one afternoon, one night. Everything below grows out of it.

## What every day has

- **One neighbour who needs you.** The story is about ten minutes long, with 7 choices, and 2 of them are giving moments (real donations through COH, always with a free option).
- **Errands that get done on screen.** If someone asks for something, you deliver it and see them get it, as with Day 1's bread, kunafa and Saber's two loaves.
- **Three small good deeds** around the hara, with a sparkle and **فين؟** to find them.
- **Something new in the hara**: a stall opens, a person comes back, a place unlocks. A red "جديد في الحارة" sign says what on the first visit.
- **One more lantern** on the string of 30 over the minaret, and a page of your day.

## Two kinds of days: iftar days and suhoor days

Most days run from the afternoon (asr) through iftar and taraweeh. Every week or so, a day happens at **suhoor** instead (2–4 AM), because suhoor is where Ramadan gets quiet and close:

- **The musaharati** walks the hara with his drum, calling people by name, kids hanging out of windows to hear their own names.
- Neighbours on rooftops and doorsteps eating **ful, eggs, cheese and yoghurt**; tea; the bakery's first batch at 2 AM.
- **The imsak** call, the last sip of water, then the **fajr adhan** and the hara going quiet, a blue sky over the minaret.
- The light is its own time of day: deep blue night turning grey-pink at the end of the scene.

Suhoor days: **2, 9, 16, 23, and 27** (Laylat al-Qadr, when the whole hara stays up until fajr).

**On every suhoor night, Sayed's ful corner becomes a party: خيمة عم سيد** (built, `js/hara-sahra.js`). A big khayamiya tent with music and confetti coming out of the door, and inside a band, a tanoura dancer whose skirt lights up when the house lights go down, the aragoz puppet show, a drinks bar, and the whole hara on floor cushions eating ful. Each suhoor day's story can send you in there for a scene. Preview: `walk.html?day=2&time=suhoor`.

### Locked places: curiosity all month

Some places sit in the hara from Day 1 with their doors shut, so people keep wondering what's behind them (built, `js/hara-places.js` and one file each). Three ways in:

| Opens | Place | The story behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Key, day 4 (again day 9) | برج الحمام, Hagg Abdo's pigeon tower | Take the key to Nemat; she climbs up for the first time since he died and gives you a pigeon that follows you all month |
| Errand, from day 6 | بيت الأستاذ نبيل, the plain lilac house | Return his keys; inside is a pool and a beach. His letters show he's the hara's secret giver (the mercy table, the hung bread). You keep his secret |
| Night 15 | ملاهي ليالي رمضان, the fair | Games for tickets, rides, the big wheel over the whole hara; 60 tickets buy the paper cone hat you wear all month |
| Night 22 | سينما النصر, the cinema | الحارة زمان, Morsy's film of Ramadan 1999: the neighbours see their young selves, and the first mercy table, laid by Nabil's mother |
| Key, day 18 (again day 24) | دكان عم شوقي, the toy shop | Mend three old toys with Shawky and take them to three kids who'd have nothing for Eid |

Each player's key hides in one of a few spots on its day; the errands list gives a riddle, not a marker. Missing a key is told plainly when you next come out, with its second-chance day; missing both keeps that place shut (and on Eid the toy shop's three kids stand at its window). When the daily stories are written, a day's story can point at these (Day 4's neighbour mentions the old tower, Day 22's ends at the cinema), but none of them depends on a story.

## Things you buy that stay with you

Items you buy stay yours for the rest of the month: you wear them, carry them, or see them on your house.

| From day | Item | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Your own tin fanoos, in colours you pick | In your hand every night |
| 8 | A string of lights and a khayamiya cloth for your balcony | On your house in the hara, every night after |
| 10 | Seeds for the garden | Your plant grows a little every day until Eid |
| 12 | A wooden lantern from Saber, with your name carved on it | Replaces your tin fanoos |
| 14 | A new Ramadan galabeya (boy) or abaya and hijab (girl) | What you wear from then on |
| 19 | A misbaha or a prayer rug | Your spot in the mosque, and at home |
| 22 | A tray of kahk for mama | On the iftar table, and mama's line changes |
| 28 | Eid clothes | You wear them on Eid morning |
| 15 | The fair's paper cone hat (won with fair tickets, built) | On your head everywhere but the mosque |
| 4 | Your pigeon from Abdo's tower (a gift, built) | Following you round the hara, on your shoulder at night |

**How you pay for them (decided).** You earn coins from the good deeds around the hara, and spend them on these things in the game. Coins are never bought with real money: real money in this game only ever goes to EFB's campaigns through the giving moments, so giving never feels like a shop.

## People you helped come back

Every person you help returns later in a new role, so the month feels like one story:

- Saber (Day 1) reopens his workshop on Day 12 and makes the lanterns for Eid.
- Om Youssef (Day 2) runs her qatayef table from Day 7, and gives you your first plate free.
- Rizq, who tried to buy Saber's tools, gets his own day (around Day 11) when his shop floods, and you decide how he's treated.
- Hassan's board of hung bread fills up across the month, one paper for every bread someone paid for.
